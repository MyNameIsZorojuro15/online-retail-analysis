Online retailer: two-year sales review

UK online gift wholesaler, Dec 2009 – Dec 2011, 1,067,371 transaction lines (UCI Online Retail II, CC BY 4.0). Comparisons use two full 12-month windows; December 2011 stops on the 9th and is left out of trends.

Revenue is flat to slightly up, and a third of the year is earned in September to November.

Growth came from bigger baskets, not more orders.

UK revenue moved -0.3 %, so the growth came from abroad. Netherlands +2 %, Germany +12 %, France +41 %, Australia +364 %, Spain +32 %; Ireland fell 26 %.

About one in five new customers buys again in any given later month.

28 % of customers bring 70 % of revenue; the at-risk group is the cheapest revenue to win back.

Cancellations are small overall but spike in some months; worth a per-product look.

What I would do next quarter

  1. Win back the at-risk group. 736 customers who used to order often have not ordered for a median of 142 days; they spent £812k last year. A targeted offer to them costs less than finding new customers.
  2. Plan stock and staff for September to November. 38 % of the year's revenue lands in those three months, 15 % in November alone.
  3. Grow order count, not only basket size. Orders fell 4.0 % while average order value rose 5.7 %; orders per customer fell 4.4 %. A reorder reminder for frequent buyers addresses that directly.
  4. Find out why Ireland fell. It is the second-largest market abroad and dropped 26 % while the UK was flat; check whether a few large buyers left before spending on new markets.
  5. Fix the order-entry controls. 2,662 lines were sales reversed within a day, including one order of 74,215 units; a quantity or price sanity check at entry would stop most of them.
